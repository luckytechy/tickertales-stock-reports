What's happening

Q1 FY27 results (reported 31 Jul 2026) showed standalone PAT down 27% year-on-year and cigarette segment PBIT down 35%, after the government replaced the compensation cess on cigarettes with a 40% GST-on-MRP plus specific excise duty structure from 1 Feb 2026. Q2 FY27 results are expected around late October 2026, though the exact date is not yet officially confirmed. Separately, a large block deal of roughly 2.9% of equity (~₹9,437 crore) changed hands on 8 October 2026 with counterparties undisclosed, and global investor GQG Partners trimmed its stake from 3.48% to 3.17%.

In plain terms. ITC earns most of its profit from cigarettes but has spent two decades building a separate, fast-growing packaged food and personal care business. In February 2026 the government changed how cigarettes are taxed, and that single change - not falling demand - knocked a big chunk off profit this quarter. On top of that, ITC spun its hotels business off into its own listed company in January 2025, so comparing today's ITC share price to its old history isn't quite apples-to-apples. The real tension: is this a cash-rich, well-run business that just absorbed a one-off tax shock, or does cigarette taxation keep changing in ways that will recur and structurally cap this profit pool?

The quarterly story

Q1 FY27 (June 2026 quarter): standalone PAT of ₹3,579 crore, down 27% YoY; EBITDA down 28%. Cigarettes - the dominant profit segment - saw net revenue fall 25% and PBIT fall 35% (margin down roughly 900 basis points), driven entirely by the Feb 2026 GST/excise overhaul; management pushed through 30-plus pricing and pack-size changes to protect volumes rather than letting demand collapse. FMCG-Others (foods, personal care, etc.) grew revenue 12% (16% excluding staples) with PBIT up 21% - healthy, margin-accretive growth, though still a smaller profit pool than cigarettes. Paperboards revenue grew 9% with PBIT up 38%. Agri revenue fell 17% on a reported basis but the company says it was up 9% on an underlying/like-for-like basis due to a deficient monsoon - flagging this as a company-adjusted figure, not independently verified. Hotels do not appear in either this quarter or the year-ago quarter because of the January 2025 demerger, so that part of the YoY comparison is clean. Q2 FY27 numbers are not yet out.

Is it cheap or pricey?

At roughly 16.3-16.5x trailing earnings, ITC trades well below its own historical range (which has run 25-30x) and below FMCG peers like HUL - statistically cheap on an earnings basis. Why it's cheap: the market is pricing in recurring regulatory/tax risk to the cigarette profit pool (roughly 80% of PBIT), not a broken business. Whether it is actually cheap depends on whether February 2026's tax restructuring was a one-time reset - the case Citi made in its October 2026 upgrade (target ₹300, citing ~75% tax pass-through already achieved) - or the first of further hikes, as some brokerages argue given illicit-trade volumes estimated at up to a third of the legal cigarette market. On book value (P/B ~4.5x) and returns (ROE ~29%, 3-year average ~35%) it is not cheap at all - this is an earnings-multiple de-rating tied to tax-policy uncertainty, not a balance-sheet discount.

Is the business growing?

Mixed by segment. The cigarette decline in Q1 FY27 is tax-led, not volume-led - the company protected underlying volumes through pricing and pack-size changes, so this is margin compression from an external (tax) source rather than demand erosion. FMCG-Others growth (revenue +12%, PBIT +21%) looks closer to genuine volume-and-mix-led growth, helped by digital-first brands and a cloud-kitchen business whose order volumes rose 90% YoY - margin is expanding here too, but off real scale gains rather than cost-cutting alone. Agri's decline was weather-driven (deficient monsoon), flagged as cyclical rather than structural.

Do profits turn into cash?

Profits convert reliably into cash: ROCE is roughly 39%, debt is near-zero (a small ₹2,399 crore of borrowings as of March 2026 is immaterial for a company this size), and the ~75% dividend payout is funded from genuine free cash flow rather than borrowing. The risk here is business/regulatory, not financial - unpredictable cigarette taxation and a large illicit-trade segment eating into the formal market, not balance-sheet stress.

Who owns it

ITC has no traditional promoter - it is institutionally and professionally run rather than founder/family-controlled, which is itself worth noting since most Indian large-caps are promoter-driven. British American Tobacco (BAT) is the largest shareholder at 22.91% as of June 2026, down from roughly 29% before a series of stake sales in 2024-25; BAT functions as a strategic/financial investor, not a promoter, and there is no pledging since no promoter category exists. FII holding is roughly 34% and DII roughly 49% - that DII figure looks unusually high against ITC's own history and could not be independently cross-checked, so treat it as unverified pending the next official filing. Notable investor GQG Partners recently trimmed its stake from 3.48% to 3.17%, and an undisclosed counterparty block deal moved about 2.9% of equity on 8 October 2026 - worth watching in the next shareholding disclosure.
