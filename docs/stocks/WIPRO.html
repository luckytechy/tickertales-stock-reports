What's happening

Wipro reports Q2 FY27 (quarter ended September 2026) results on 15 October 2026 - two days from now - with management commentary on the call the same evening. The company had guided IT services dollar revenue of $2.574-2.627bn for the quarter, implying just -1.5% to +0.5% constant-currency sequential growth, a soft band. Separately, Wipro completed the merger of its VLSI design unit into its IT services arm (4 Oct 2026) and closed a small bolt-on acquisition of customer contracts from Alpha Net Consulting (1 Oct 2026), alongside reported new AI and cybersecurity deal wins.

In plain terms. Wipro gets paid by large companies abroad to run their IT systems, write software and handle back-office processes - it earns mostly in dollars but reports in rupees. The tension: reported rupee revenue grew a healthy-looking 10.6% last quarter, but that was flattered by a weaker rupee - the underlying dollar/constant-currency business barely grew, and profit was flat. At the same time, operating margin slipped to a 15-quarter low because the company is spending more on people and strategic bets. So the headline growth number and the real growth number are telling different stories.

The quarterly story

Q4 FY26 (Mar-2026): IT services revenue $2,651m, +0.6% QoQ/+2.1% YoY; operating margin 17.3%; FY26 full-year margin 17.2% (up 0.2pp YoY) - a reasonably steady year. Q1 FY27 (Jun-2026): consolidated revenue ₹24,479cr, +10.6% YoY, but net profit was flat at ₹3,352cr (+0.6% YoY) - the gap between a double-digit rupee revenue rise and flat profit is largely currency translation (rupee depreciation) plus margin compression, not real volume growth. IT services operating margin fell to 16.0%, its lowest in 15 quarters, down from 17.3% a quarter earlier. CFO Aparna Iyer said these are deliberate investments in people and strategic priority areas that 'may create some near-term margin volatility' but should build a 'strong foundation for future growth.' Large-deal bookings rose to $1,626m (+12.9% QoQ CC), continuing a pickup from $1,440m (+65.1% QoQ CC) in Q4 FY26 - the order pipeline looks healthier than the revenue line currently shows. EPS of ₹3.20 for Q1 FY27 reflects the post-split share count (see below) and isn't directly comparable to pre-December-2024 EPS figures.

Is it cheap or pricey?

At roughly 12x trailing earnings and about 2x book value against an ROE near 16% and ROCE near 18%, Wipro is not expensive next to its own profitability ratios, and it trades at a discount to larger peers like TCS. Why it is cheap: the market is pricing in near-zero underlying (constant-currency) revenue growth, a margin that just hit a 15-quarter low, and sector-wide fears that AI will shrink the traditional IT-outsourcing pie. Whether it is cheap depends on whether that margin stabilises and deal-booking momentum (up sharply over the last two quarters) eventually converts into revenue - if growth stays flat, the multiple has room to compress further rather than re-rate.

Is the business growing?

Underlying growth is weak and not clearly margin-led either. IT services dollar revenue grew only 2.1% YoY in Q4 FY26 and guidance for Q2 FY27 implies another flat-to-down constant-currency quarter. The 10.6% YoY rupee revenue growth reported in Q1 FY27 is substantially a currency effect rather than real volume or pricing gains. Meanwhile margin is falling, not rising (16.0% in Q1 FY27 vs 17.2% for full-year FY26) - so this is the weakest combination per the business: close to flat volume and a shrinking margin, rather than either real volume growth or margin-led profitability.

Do profits turn into cash?

Profits are converting into cash reasonably well - Q1 FY27 operating cash flow was 98% of net income. The balance sheet carries net cash against relatively low debt (reported net cash materially higher than reported total debt), so financial risk - the risk of the company itself getting into trouble over borrowings - is low. The real risk here is business risk: soft dollar revenue growth, margin pressure from investment spending, and sector-wide uncertainty about AI's effect on IT-services demand, not balance-sheet fragility.

Who owns it

Promoter holding (Azim Premji and family entities via Zash Traders, Prazim Traders and Hasham Traders) stands at about 72.6% - among the highest promoter concentration of any large listed Indian IT company - with zero pledged shares, a clean signal on that front. FIIs hold about 10.8%, mutual funds about 4.3%, other domestic institutions about 3.6%, and retail/others about 8.7%. There is no marquee outside investor (no large well-known fund or anchor holder) visible on the register beyond routine institutional participants; this is overwhelmingly a promoter-controlled stock with a thin free float.

Fundamental quality: Moderate

On paper the business is sound - ROE near 16%, ROCE near 18%, a net-cash balance sheet, no pledging - but that has not translated into growth or shareholder returns: dollar revenue is barely moving, margin just hit a multi-year low, and the stock has underperformed the Nifty across every time horizon and sits roughly half below its 2022 peak. Quality metrics and shareholder outcomes diverge here, and both facts need to be held together rather than one overriding the other.
